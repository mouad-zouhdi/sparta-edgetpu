=====================================================================
A. LES TROIS PLUS PROBABLES
=====================================================================

---------------------------------------------------------------------
Q1. "Your x-axis goes up to a utilization of 4. How can a task set with
     utilization 4 run on a single processor?"
---------------------------------------------------------------------

REPONSE
"The utilization on the x-axis is computed with the unpruned models. It is
the load the system would have if every network ran at full size. Pruning
then replaces each model by a smaller and faster version, so the utilization
that is actually scheduled is always below one. A point at utilization 4
means the original system was four times too heavy, and we can still make
about 17 percent of those task sets fit on one accelerator."

[FR] C'est LA question qui va tomber, parce que le graphe a l'air impossible :
     aucun systeme ne peut avoir une utilisation de 4 sur un processeur.
     Tout repose sur le fait que l'axe est l'utilisation AVANT elagage.
     Si vous ne dites que ca, c'est deja suffisant. Le chiffre 17 % se lit
     directement sur votre courbe rouge a x=4.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
Q2. "Why non-preemptive EDF? Preemptive EDF schedules strictly more."
---------------------------------------------------------------------

REPONSE
"Because on this accelerator a preemption is very expensive. If we interrupt
an inference to run another model, the weights of that other model have to be
loaded into the 8 megabyte on-chip memory. We measured that load, it is the
gap between the first and the second inference in my earlier table, and it is
around 25 milliseconds. That is more than most of our inference times. So
non-preemptive EDF does lose some schedulability, but it avoids paying that
cost at every preemption."

[FR] Vous avez le chiffre sur votre propre slide 4 : ResNet-18 fait 36,9 ms
     a la premiere inference contre 11,5 ensuite, soit 25 ms d'ecart. C'est
     exactement pour ca que vous avez dit "keep this in mind" a l'oral.
     Renvoyez a cette slide, c'est votre meilleur argument.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
Q3. "You run four different models on one TPU that has only 8 megabytes.
     Doesn't every job have to reload its weights?"
---------------------------------------------------------------------

REPONSE
"It does, and that is exactly why the execution times we use in the scheduling
experiments are the first-inference times, the ones that include the weight
transfer. We do not assume that the model is already resident. Non-preemption
is a separate matter: it guarantees that a single inference is never cut in
half, which would force a second reload inside the same job."

[FR] Votre encadrant confirme que ce sont bien les temps de premiere inference
     qui ont ete utilises. C'est le choix sur : dans le pire cas, un autre
     modele a occupe la SRAM depuis le job precedent de la tache, donc chaque
     job demarre a froid. Parfois un job retrouvera ses poids en place et ira
     plus vite, mais une analyse pire cas ne peut pas compter dessus.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
Q4. "Is the non-preemption at the job level or at the task level?"
---------------------------------------------------------------------

REPONSE
"At the job level. Once an inference starts it runs to completion, and the
scheduler only makes a decision when the processor becomes idle. There is no
batching of several jobs of the same task."

[FR] C'est la definition donnee dans le papier : "every job executes from its
     start uninterruptedly until completion". Il n'existe pas de non-preemption
     "au grain de la tache" en ordonnancement temps reel classique.

     Et surtout : avec des echeances implicites, une tache n'a JAMAIS deux jobs
     en attente en meme temps. Le job k doit finir avant T_i, et le job k+1
     n'arrive qu'a partir de T_i. Donc des jobs d'une meme tache ne peuvent pas
     s'enchainer pour amortir un seul chargement de poids : entre deux, il y a
     au moins une periode, pendant laquelle les autres taches s'executent et
     ecrasent la SRAM.

     Les deux choix repondent a deux questions differentes, ils ne se
     remplacent pas :
       - temps a froid  -> combien coute UN job, en supposant le pire ?
       - non-preemption -> ce job peut-il etre coupe, ce qui forcerait un
                           second rechargement a l'interieur du meme job ?

=====================================================================
B. SUR LA METHODE
=====================================================================

---------------------------------------------------------------------
Q5. "Is your schedulability test exact?"
---------------------------------------------------------------------

REPONSE
"No, it is a sufficient test, from Jeffay and co-authors. It is a simple
utilization-based condition and it runs in polynomial time. If it says yes,
the task set is definitely schedulable. But some sets that it rejects would
actually be schedulable, so our results are pessimistic: what we report is a
lower bound on what pruning can buy."

[FR] "Sufficient" veut dire : pas de faux positif, mais des faux negatifs.
     C'est en votre faveur, insistez la-dessus : la vraie courbe rouge serait
     au-dessus de celle que vous montrez.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
Q6. "Is your branch-and-bound optimal, or is it a heuristic?"
---------------------------------------------------------------------

REPONSE
"It is optimal for the problem as we state it. The two cuts are safe: we only
discard a branch when we can prove that nothing inside it can be schedulable,
or that nothing inside it can beat the best solution we already have. So it
visits fewer nodes than a full enumeration, but it returns the same optimum."

[FR] Point important : "branch-and-bound" n'est pas une heuristique. Les deux
     elagages sont des preuves, pas des paris. Le premier s'appuie sur le fait
     que moins elaguer ne peut qu'allonger les temps ; le second sur le fait
     que la meilleure precision atteignable dans la branche est deja calculee.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
Q7. "Why write a branch-and-bound instead of feeding the ILP to a solver?"
---------------------------------------------------------------------

REPONSE
"The ILP is the formulation, the branch-and-bound is how we solve it. Our tree
follows the structure of the problem, one level per task, and the
schedulability condition we use can be checked incrementally, so we can test a
partial assignment as soon as it is made. We did not benchmark it against a
commercial solver, and that would be a fair comparison to add."

[FR] Ne pretendez pas que c'est plus rapide qu'un solveur, ce n'est pas mesure
     dans le papier. Reconnaitre la comparaison manquante est plus solide que
     de bluffer.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
Q8. "How long does it take to solve?"
---------------------------------------------------------------------

REPONSE
"It is a design-time decision, computed once before deployment, so solving
time is not a real constraint. In the worst case the tree has nine to the
power six leaves for our experiments, but the two cuts remove most of it. We
do not report timings in the paper."

[FR] 9 taux d'elagage, 6 taches, donc au pire 531 441 feuilles. C'est petit
     pour un calcul hors ligne.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
Q9. "What exactly is the accuracy you maximize?"
---------------------------------------------------------------------

REPONSE
"It is the sum over the tasks of the accuracy divided by the period. So a task
that runs more often weighs more in the objective, which reflects that it
produces more results per second. It is not a plain average of the accuracies."

[FR] Nuance a connaitre : votre slide dit "maximize accuracy", quelqu'un peut
     demander laquelle. La ponderation par 1/T est visible dans l'objectif du
     modele que vous affichez.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
Q10. "What is the blocking factor, and why do you need it?"
---------------------------------------------------------------------

REPONSE
"It exists because we never interrupt an inference. A job can arrive just
after a longer and less urgent inference has started, and then it has to wait
for that one to finish even though it is more urgent. B k is the longest such
wait, that is the largest inference time among the tasks that have a longer
period. It is the price of non-preemption, and it shows up as an extra term
in the test."

---------------------------------------------------------------------
Q11. "What happens if a task set is not schedulable even with maximum
      pruning?"
---------------------------------------------------------------------

REPONSE
"Then we declare it unschedulable and return nothing. The algorithm starts by
testing the assignment where every task is pruned as much as possible. If that
one fails, no other assignment can work, since anything else is slower."


=====================================================================
C. SUR LES EXPERIENCES ET LES CHIFFRES
=====================================================================

---------------------------------------------------------------------
Q12. "How do you generate the task sets?"
---------------------------------------------------------------------

REPONSE
"With RandFixedSum, which is the standard way in our community to draw task
utilizations that sum exactly to a target value without biasing the
distribution. Six tasks per set, 250 sets per utilization point, periods drawn
log-uniformly between 10 and 100 milliseconds, and each task takes one of the
accuracy and speedup profiles we measured in the benchmark."

---------------------------------------------------------------------
Q13. "Why is the accuracy loss only 2 percent, even at utilization 4?"
---------------------------------------------------------------------

REPONSE
"Because of the shape of the two curves from the first half of the talk.
Accuracy stays almost flat up to about 60 percent of parameters removed, while
the latency gain is largest right around that point, when the model starts
fitting on chip. So the optimizer can take very large speedups in the region
where accuracy is still cheap, and it only has to go beyond that region for
the most loaded task sets."

[FR] C'est le lien entre vos deux moities. Bonne question a recevoir, elle
     vous permet de rappeler le message central.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
Q14. "Two percent compared to what?"
---------------------------------------------------------------------

REPONSE
"Compared to the accuracy of the same task set with the unpruned models,
including the task sets that were not schedulable without pruning."

---------------------------------------------------------------------
Q15. "Where do the C values come from? Are they real worst-case execution
      times?"
---------------------------------------------------------------------

REPONSE
"They are measured on the device. For the scheduling experiments we use the
first-inference times, which include the weight transfer, and we take the
worst case observed. The accelerator helps
here: there is no cache and no branch prediction, and the sequence of
operations is fixed at compile time, so the spread between inferences is
small. It is a measurement-based worst case, not a formal bound."

[FR] Ne dites pas "WCET" au sens analytique, ce serait attaquable. "Measured
     worst case" est exact et honnete.

---------------------------------------------------------------------
Q16. "Your scenario is a drone, but the models are trained on CIFAR-100.
      Is that realistic?"
---------------------------------------------------------------------

REPONSE
"The scenario is illustrative, and we say so. The timing constraints and the
inference times are real measurements on the accelerator, but the networks
were trained on CIFAR-100, not on a weed dataset. What the scenario
demonstrates is the mechanism, not an agronomic result."


=====================================================================
D. QUESTIONS COURTES
=====================================================================

Q17. "Is the pruning rate changed at runtime?"
"No. One version per task is selected offline and stays fixed. There is no
runtime overhead and no mode change to analyze."

Q18. "Why sporadic tasks, and why implicit deadlines?"
"Sporadic means we only assume a minimum time between two activations, which
is the realistic model for event-triggered perception. Implicit deadline means
each job must finish before the next activation can arrive. It is the standard
setting, and the test we use is stated for it."

Q19. "Why six tasks?"
"It is a representative size for an embedded perception workload. The method
does not depend on it."

Q20. "Does this extend to multiple TPUs?"
"Not directly, the schedulability condition we use is for a single processing
unit. Extending the benchmark and the analysis to multi-TPU systems is our
main line of future work."
